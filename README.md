# Customer Segmentation Using RFM Analysis and K-Means Clustering

## Project Overview

This project uses **RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis and K-Means clustering** to identify distinct customer segments based on their purchasing behaviour.

Using transactional data from the Online Retail II dataset, the project transforms historical purchase records into customer-level behavioural features, applies unsupervised machine learning to identify customer groups, and translates the findings into actionable business recommendations.

The objective is to help businesses better understand their customers, identify high-value segments, recognise customers who may be disengaging, and develop targeted marketing and retention strategies.

## Business Problem

Businesses often treat their entire customer base in the same way, despite significant differences in purchasing behaviour and customer value.

Some customers purchase frequently and generate substantial revenue, while others purchase occasionally or have not made a purchase for an extended period.

Without a structured approach to customer segmentation, businesses may struggle to:

- Identify and retain their most valuable customers.
- Recognise customers whose purchasing activity is declining.
- Identify opportunities to increase customer purchasing frequency.
- Allocate marketing resources effectively.
- Develop targeted customer engagement and retention strategies.

This project addresses these challenges by grouping customers according to their historical purchasing behaviour and evaluating the commercial contribution of each segment.

## Project Objectives

The main objectives were to:

1. Develop customer-level RFM features from transactional data.
2. Explore customer purchasing behaviour and assess the distribution of RFM variables.
3. Apply logarithmic transformation and feature standardisation to prepare the data for clustering.
4. Use K-Means clustering to identify distinct customer groups.
5. Evaluate different clustering configurations using inertia and silhouette scores.
6. Profile the resulting customer segments using their original RFM metrics.
7. Quantify each segment's contribution to the customer base and total revenue.
8. Develop segment-specific business recommendations.

## Project Workflow

The project is organised into three notebooks.

### 1. RFM Feature Engineering

**Notebook:** `01_Segmentation_Project_rfm_features.ipynb`

The first notebook transforms transaction-level data into a customer-level RFM dataset.

The three RFM metrics are:

- **Recency:** Number of days since the customer's last purchase.
- **Frequency:** Number of unique invoices associated with the customer.
- **Monetary:** Total revenue generated by the customer.

The snapshot date was determined using the latest transaction date in the dataset plus one day.

The resulting dataset contained:

- 5,878 unique customers.
- Three behavioural features: Recency, Frequency and Monetary.
- No missing values in the resulting RFM dataset.

The output was saved as `rfm.csv`.

### 2. Exploratory Data Analysis and Feature Preparation

**Notebook:** `02_Explore_and_Prepare.ipynb`

The second notebook explored the distributions of the RFM variables and prepared them for K-Means clustering.

The analysis identified substantial positive skewness, particularly in Frequency and Monetary.

Initial skewness:

| Feature | Skewness Before Transformation |
|---|---:|
| Recency | 0.89 |
| Frequency | 12.64 |
| Monetary | 25.07 |

Applied logarithmic transformation using `np.log1p()` to reduce the influence of extreme values.

Skewness after transformation:

| Feature | Skewness After Transformation |
|---|---:|
| Recency | -0.49 |
| Frequency | 1.00 |
| Monetary | 0.27 |

The transformed features were then standardised using `StandardScaler`.

Standardisation ensured that the three features had approximately zero means and unit standard deviations, making them more suitable for distance-based clustering.

The resulting dataset was saved as `rfm_scaled.csv`.

### 3. Customer Segmentation Using K-Means

**Notebook:** `03_Kmeans_Clustering.ipynb`

The final notebook applied K-Means clustering to the prepared RFM dataset.

The analysis involved:

- Testing cluster configurations from K=2 to K=10.
- Evaluating inertia using the Elbow Method.
- Calculating silhouette scores to assess cluster separation.
- Comparing customer profiles across different cluster configurations.
- Selecting a five-cluster solution for business interpretation.
- Profiling the resulting customer groups using the original RFM metrics.
- Assigning business-oriented segment names.
- Calculating customer and revenue contributions.
- Developing segment-specific business recommendations.

## Clustering Methodology and Model Evaluation

Two evaluation measures were used to assess the clustering configurations.

### Inertia (Within-Cluster Sum of Squares)

Inertia measures the total squared distance between customers and their assigned cluster centroids.

Lower inertia indicates more compact clusters.

The Elbow Method was used to examine how inertia changed as the number of clusters increased.

### Silhouette Score

The Silhouette Score measures how well customers fit within their assigned clusters compared with other clusters.

Scores closer to +1 indicate better-defined clusters, while values around zero suggest overlapping clusters.

### Cluster Evaluation Results

| Number of Clusters | Inertia | Silhouette Score |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 8,588.985 | 0.439 |
| 3 | 6,354.343 | 0.348 |
| 4 | 4,921.233 | 0.365 |
| 5 | 4,099.106 | 0.342 |
| 6 | 3,554.696 | 0.335 |
| 7 | 3,194.501 | 0.307 |
| 8 | 2,902.434 | 0.303 |
| 9 | 2,656.562 | 0.293 |
| 10 | 2,467.378 | 0.291 |

### Selection of Five Clusters

Although K=2 produced the highest silhouette score of 0.439, the five-cluster configuration was selected because it provided more detailed and commercially useful differentiation between customer groups.

The selected five-cluster model produced:

- Inertia: 4,099.106
- Silhouette Score: 0.342

The decision prioritised business interpretability and actionable customer segmentation rather than selecting a cluster configuration based solely on the highest silhouette score.

## Customer Segments and Profiles

The final K-Means model identified five customer segments based on their purchasing behaviour.

| Segment | Customers | Avg. Recency | Avg. Frequency | Avg. Spend |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| VIP Champions | 458 | 18 days | 33 | £20,818 |
| New / Promising | 1,294 | 49 days | 9 | £3,674 |
| Need Attention | 1,143 | 33 days | 3 | £683 |
| At Risk | 1,363 | 294 days | 4 | £1,386 |
| Lost | 1,620 | 416 days | 1 | £258 |

*Average values are rounded.*

### 1. VIP Champions

These customers purchase frequently, have purchased recently and generate substantial revenue.

They represent the most commercially valuable customer group.

### 2. New / Promising

These customers demonstrate relatively strong purchasing activity and provide opportunities to increase purchasing frequency and develop long-term loyalty.

### 3. Need Attention

These customers have purchased relatively recently but demonstrate lower purchasing frequency and spending.

They represent an opportunity for increased engagement and additional purchases.

### 4. At Risk

These customers have relatively long periods since their last purchase, suggesting declining engagement and a potential risk of losing them.

### 5. Lost

These customers have very low purchasing frequency and have not purchased for an extended period.

They contribute relatively little to total revenue and may require a cost-conscious reactivation strategy.

## Key Business Findings

One of the most significant findings was the difference between customer numbers and revenue contribution across the five segments.

### Customer and Revenue Contribution

| Segment | % Customers | Total Revenue | % Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| VIP Champions | 7.8% | £9,534,457 | 54.9% |
| New / Promising | 22.0% | £4,753,523 | 27.4% |
| Need Attention | 19.4% | £780,151 | 4.5% |
| At Risk | 23.2% | £1,888,918 | 10.9% |
| Lost | 27.6% | £417,756 | 2.4% |

### Key Insights

**1. Revenue is concentrated among a small group of customers.**

VIP Champions represent only 7.8% of customers but generate 54.9% of total revenue.

This highlights the importance of retaining high-value customers and maintaining their purchasing activity.

**2. New / Promising customers represent a significant growth opportunity.**

This segment accounts for 22.0% of customers and contributes 27.4% of revenue.

Developing purchasing frequency and customer loyalty within this group could support future revenue growth.

**3. The Lost segment represents a substantial proportion of the customer base.**

Although Lost customers account for 27.6% of customers, they generate only 2.4% of total revenue.

This suggests that reactivation efforts should be carefully targeted and proportionate to their commercial value.

**4. At Risk customers require re-engagement.**

This group represents 23.2% of customers and contributes 10.9% of revenue.

Their relatively high recency values suggest an opportunity to re-engage customers before they become inactive for longer periods.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the customer profiles and revenue contribution, the following actions are recommended:

| Customer Segment | Recommended Business Action |
|---|---|
| VIP Champions | Dedicated account management, early access to new stock and loyalty rewards. |
| New / Promising | Welcome programmes, cross-selling and second-order incentives to strengthen purchasing habits. |
| Need Attention | Personalised offers and product recommendations to increase purchasing frequency. |
| At Risk | Personalised win-back contact and time-limited reactivation offers. |
| Lost | Low-cost automated communication, with limited investment in reactivation. |

These recommendations provide a basis for allocating customer engagement and retention resources according to customer behaviour and commercial value.

## Limitations and Further Improvements

Although the five-cluster solution provides useful business insights, several limitations should be considered:

- The five-cluster solution has a silhouette score of 0.342, indicating some overlap between customer groups.
- The choice of five clusters prioritises business interpretability rather than the highest silhouette score.
- RFM analysis focuses on historical purchasing behaviour and does not incorporate additional information such as customer demographics, product preferences or acquisition channels.
- Segment names are business interpretations of the cluster profiles and should not be treated as independently verified customer characteristics.
- The recommendations are proposed actions. Their actual impact would need to be assessed through targeted campaigns and controlled experiments.

Potential improvements include testing additional clustering algorithms, monitoring customer movement between segments over time, and evaluating the effectiveness of segment-specific marketing campaigns.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how transactional data can be transformed into actionable customer intelligence using RFM analysis and unsupervised machine learning.

The analysis identified five distinct customer segments and revealed a significant concentration of revenue among a relatively small group of high-value customers. VIP Champions accounted for only 7.8% of the customer base but generated 54.9% of total revenue, while the Lost segment represented 27.6% of customers but contributed only 2.4% of revenue.

These findings highlight the importance of understanding differences in customer purchasing behaviour and adopting targeted engagement strategies rather than treating all customers uniformly.

The resulting segmentation provides a data-driven foundation for customer retention, targeted marketing, loyalty initiatives and more effective allocation of commercial resources.

Overall, the project demonstrates an end-to-end analytical workflow, from data preparation and feature engineering to clustering, interpretation and business recommendations.
